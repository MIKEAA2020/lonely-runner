1. THE QUESTION, MADE PRECISE
-----------------------------
Advisor's phrasing: "Let S be a core with gap g, and let x be a filler that
is killed by S but not clean at any level-g time.  What is gap(S u {x})?
Is there a formula?  Is the drop bounded below by something like 1/(|S|+2)?"

In the X(S) framework (Theorem G, "silent killers" audit): at the FIXED
level g (e.g. 1/(m+1)) below gap(S), the trichotomy is kill-and-clean
(lands exactly at g), killed-not-clean = SILENT KILLER (drops below g), and
unkilled (stays above).  The NSK census (1.71B cores, m=2..10) found ZERO
silent killers at the LRC levels.  That census measured the drop only at
the single fixed level g = 1/(m+1).

The complementary regime -- and the substance of the advisor's question --
is the drop from the core's OWN level: g = gap(S).  There every x is
trivially "killed" (S has no escapes above its own gap), so the trichotomy
collapses to a dichotomy:

  TOUCH:    some argmax time t* of S has ||x t*|| >= gap(S)   -> gap preserved;
  NO-TOUCH: ||x t*|| < gap(S) at every argmax time            -> gap DROPS.

The drop question: how far does it fall, where does it land, and is the
landing a function of S's own level structure or of x's arithmetic?
This is not a re-labeling of the classification program: the drop question
at level 1/(k+2) IS the induction step of LRC (Section 6).


2. THEOREM D (THE DROP FORMULA) -- the answer to "is there a formula?"
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Let S be a finite set of positive integers, x not in S, and write
  q(t) = min_{v in S} ||v t||,   p(t) = ||x t||,   h = min(p, q).
Then gap(S u {x}) = sup_t h(t), and:

(i) MONOTONICITY + TOUCH.  gap(S u {x}) <= gap(S), with equality iff x
    touches the argmax of S.  (If x misses every argmax time, continuity
    of p and compactness of the complement of small argmax-neighborhoods
    give a strict drop.)

(ii) THE DICHOTOMY.  In a drop case, every t_new in Argmax(S u {x}) is
    exactly one of
      (C) CROSSING:  p(t_new) = q(t_new) = gap(S u {x});
      (Q) Q-CORNER:  q(t_new) = gap(S u {x}) < p(t_new), and then t_new is
                     automatically a local maximum of q [p > q on a
                     neighborhood, h = q there, and a global max of h
                     restricts to a local max of q];
      (P) P-CORNER:  p(t_new) = 1/2 = gap(S u {x}) -- impossible in a drop
                     case (would force gap(S u {x}) = 1/2 >= gap(S)).

(iii) THE FORMULA.  gap(S u {x}) = max(Q*, C*) where
      Q* = max{ q(t) : t a local max of q with p(t) >= q(t) }  (S's OWN
             critical ladder, cleared by x),
      C* = the best crossing value                                (mixed
             arithmetic of x with S).
    In particular: a drop attained at a q-corner lands EXACTLY on S's own
    ladder (Q* = gap(S u {x})); a drop attained at a crossing is carried
    by the mixed arithmetic.  The local maxima of q are computable on the
    full breakpoint grid {j/(2v)} u {a/(v_i +- v_j)} (valleys included!),
    on which q is piecewise linear -- the gap solver's candidate list
    (peaks + crossings only) is NOT sufficient for local-max detection.

LEMMA (RESIDUE LAW).  If u != w both bind at t = a/b (lowest terms), then
    u = +-w (mod b).  Proof: ||ut|| = ||wt|| gives ut = +-wt (mod 1), so
    (u+-w)a = 0 (mod b), and gcd(a,b)=1.  Machine-checked on 28,390/28,390
    crossing binding pairs in the table.  At a floor drop t* = 1/(k+2) with
    binders {1, x}: 1 + x = 0 (mod k+2) -- the tight-extension residue
    condition; at the inter-rung landing t* = 4/23 with binders {5, 18}:
    5 + 18 = 23.

So the answer to "is there a formula?" is YES: the drop is the max of two
terms, one inherited from the core's own level structure (Q*), one mixed
with the filler (C*).  The empirical content is which term carries it, how
close to the LRC floor the max can come, and what constrains C*.

5. FINDINGS
-----------
E1  BOUNDED.  margin >= 0 on all 8,922 drops; SILENT = 0.  The drop never
    crosses the LRC floor in this universe.  105 drops land exactly ON the
    floor -- these are precisely the tight extensions (the X(S, 1/(k+2))
    fillers; e.g. {1,2,3,4}+5 -> 1/6 at t*=1/6, binders {1,5}, 1+5=6).

E2  QUANTIZED.  Above the floor the closest landings are the RUNG-2 levels:
    the minimum positive margin at aug m in {2,3,4,5,6,8} is exactly
    1/((2m+1)(m+1)), attained by S = {1,...,m-1}, x = 2m -> 2/(2m+1).  The
    INTER-RUNG WINDOW (1/(m+1), 2/(2m+1)) is EMPTY at those m in-universe:
      no drop lands strictly between the LRC floor and the second rung.
    At aug=7 the window is inhabited: S={1,2,3,4,5,7} + x=18 -> 3/23
    (crossing, t*=4/23, binders {5,18}) -- byte-for-byte the committed
    n=7 inter-rung census vector v=(1,2,3,4,5,7,18), now with its
    mechanism: a TIGHT 6-core whose filler 18 overshoots the floor.  The
    other primitive n=7 inhabitant {1,3,4,5,7,13}+18 has a core that is
    tight@6 but barren at 7 (not in the productive-core files); the n=6
    inhabitant {1,5,6,11,16,17} @ 5/33 grows from the rung-4 core
    {1,5,6,11,16} (gap 4/17), also outside the scanned set.  The inter-rung
    inhabitants are the "second excited band": drops from tight/rung cores
    that overshoot the floor, landing at non-rung crossing values.

E3  MECHANISM SPLIT.  Drops: ~80-91% crossings, ~4-15% q-corners (+mixed).
    Q-corners land exactly on S's own critical ladder (Q* = g_new, machine-
    checked on every pair).  Consecutive cores {1,...,k} have trivial
    ladders (no interior local maxima of q), so ALL their drops are
    crossings -- including the canonical floor drops {1..k}+(k+1) -> 1/(k+2)
    via the crossing at t* = 1/(k+2) with binders {1, k+1}.

E4  FLOOR STRUCTURE.  The 105 floor drops have argmax times on 1/(k+2)-type
    grids (t* = 1/(k+2), 1/(3(k+2)?) scaled), binders {1,x} or their
    scalings; the residue law holds 418/418 at floor crossings.

E5  x-DEPENDENCE.  The landing value depends strongly on x: a single core
    attains up to 30 distinct g_new values over its x-range.  The per-core
    MINIMUM over x is nonetheless structured: best drops land on zoo values
    (2/7, 3/10, 4/13, 2/9, ... -- the rung/mod ladder of the core one level
    up).  90 of 524 cores have a tight extension in range; 434 do not --
    their best drop stays strictly above the floor (all barren cores,
    by definition of barrenness, and most small cores: e.g. {1,4}'s best is
    2/7 via x=3, {1,7}'s best is 4/13 via x=6).

E6  NO CHAOS NEAR THE FLOOR.  No drop lands within 1/200 above the floor
    without landing exactly on it: the margins are quantized into the
    bands  floor / rung-2 / inter-rung inhabitants / higher.


6. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR LRC (the honest boundary)
------------------------------------------------
The statement "for every core S with |S|=k and gap(S) >= 1/(k+1), and every
x, gap(S u {x}) >= 1/(k+2)" IS the induction step of LRC (base: gap({v}) =
1/2 = 1/(1+1)); over all cores it is equivalent to LRC.  Theorem D splits
this step into two clean halves:

  (Q-half)  every cleared ladder level of every core is >= 1/(k+2):
            core-inherited structure -- classification-adjacent, and the
            empirical picture (ladders = zoo values) supports it;

  (C-half)  every crossing value of (S, x) is >= 1/(k+2):  the mixed
            arithmetic -- THIS is where the difficulty lives.  The residue
            law constrains crossings (binding pairs u,w satisfy u = +-w
            mod den(t*)) but does not yet bound them.

So the drop experiment does not prove LRC -- it LOCALIZES the residue: the
conjecture is exactly the statement that no crossing value of any admissible
(S, x) falls below 1/(k+2).  Everything else about the drop (the formula,
the dichotomy, the touch criterion, the ladder landings) is now theorem.
The quantization findings (E2) say the crossing values stay in the bands
above the floor in-universe; the second-rung conjecture (inter-rung
emptiness, verified at n=8 to V=50 and here at aug in {2..6,8}) is the
first structural statement about those bands.


7. THE SECOND LANGUAGE (the bridge prospectus, stated precisely)
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The structural facts above are all statements about finite abelian groups
in disguise.  Dictionary (t in T = R/Z; phi_v : t -> v t; K_v = ker phi_v =
{m/v}, a cyclic subgroup of order v; ||v t|| = v . dist(t, K_v)):

  arithmetic                              | compact-group
  -------------------------------------------------------------------------
  ||v t|| >= g                            | t outside N_{g/v}(K_v), the
                                          | (g/v)-neighborhood of K_v
  gap(V) >= g                             | cup_{v in V} N_{g/v}(K_v) != T
                                          | (a COVERING question: n kernel
                                          |  neighborhoods, total measure
                                          |  2ng; covering forces
                                          |  g >= 1/(2n) -- the trivial
                                          |  bound; LRC says they never
                                          |  cover at g = 1/(n+1))
  kill: [alpha,beta] in one window        | [alpha,beta] inside a single
                                          | component of N_{g/x}(K_x)
  touch at t* in L(S,g)                   | t* in the uncovered set, x's
                                          | neighborhood avoided
  binders u,w at t* = a/b                 | t* in K_{u+w} or K_{|u-w|}
                                          | (u = +-w mod b): the ARGMAX
                                          |  LIVES IN A KERNEL of the
                                          |  sum/difference subgroup --
                                          |  this is why sum-richness
                                          |  (12 = 5+7) marks the zoo
  crossing landing values                 | rational points of the kernel
                                          |  lattice: gaps on the grids
                                          |  1/(u+w) -- additive structure
  X(S) decomposition                      | extension of a character-
                                          |  avoidance set by kernel-
                                          |  neighborhood geometry

Candidate imports (none executed yet -- this is Step 1 of the bridge only):
  (a) large-sieve / Zhang-type bounds on the measure of
      {t : max_v ||v t|| <= delta}  (the covered set);
  (b) additive combinatorics on the kernel lattice (K_u cap K_w =
      K_{gcd(u,w)}: a subgroup poset; Freiman-type statements about which
      sums u+w can host argmaxes);
  (c) Fourier/uncertainty principles for the indicators of the uncovered
      set.
The honest caveat (the advisor's subtlety): this is a DUALITY dictionary
(times are characters; kernels are the dual objects), not a same-object
bridge as in the matroid-basis/log-concavity move.  The inequality needed
in the second language -- "n kernel neighborhoods at scale 1/(n+1) never
cover T for admissible speed sets" -- is a covering/sieve statement with
no known off-the-shelf proof; the dictionary above is the precise waiting
point for one.
