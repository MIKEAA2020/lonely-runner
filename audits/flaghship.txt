## Executive Summary & Appraisal

This paper presents a negative structural result concerning the **Lonely Runner Conjecture ($\mathrm{LRC}_n$)**: lossless reformulations (finite cyclic coverings, transfer-matrix cascades, character systems, and quotient-torus zonotopes) cannot be resolved by standard analytical, spectral, or polyhedral toolkits because **every natural strengthening required by those toolkits is strictly false**. The paper establishes this through exact rational counterexamples starting at $n = 5$ (e.g., covering radius $\rho \ge 97/288 > 1/3 = \mathrm{thr}_5$ for $v = (1,2,3,4,5)$).

The conceptual thesis—dubbed the **"type mismatch"**—is compelling, philosophically coherent, and backed by auditable computational provenance. However, the draft suffers from **mathematical notation collisions, missing terms in displayed definitions, orphan bibliography entries, and formatting artifacts**. 

Below is a line-level audit identifying every gap, followed by concrete, drop-in LaTeX improvements.

## I. Structural & Mathematical Gaps (Critical Priority)

### 1. Severe Notation Collision: $V$ (Speed Vector) vs. $V$ (Quotient Space)
* **Location:** Page 4 (§2.1), Page 5 (§2.2, Item 3), Page 10 (§3.5).
* **The Gap:** In §2.1, the speed vector is defined as $V = (v_1, \dots, v_n) \in \mathbb{Z}_{>0}^n$. But in §2.2 (Item 3), the text states:
  $$\text{“Let } V = \mathbb{R}^n / \mathbb{R}v, \text{ let } \pi : \mathbb{R}^n \to V \text{ be the projection}\dots \text{and let } \|\cdot\|_V \text{ be the quotient norm}\dots\text{”}$$
  Then, in the very next sentence:
  $$\text{“The }\delta\text{-form reads: the center class has quotient-norm depth at most }\mathrm{thr}_n\text{ in every } V.”$$
  Here, $V$ simultaneously denotes:
  1. An integer tuple $(v_1, \dots, v_n)$;
  2. The $(n-1)$-dimensional quotient vector space $\mathbb{R}^n / \mathbb{R}v$;
  3. The index/subscript for the quotient norm $\|\cdot\|_V$.
* **Impact:** In §3.5 and §5, $v$ and $V$ are swapped unpredictably (e.g., $\rho(v)$ vs. $\rho(V)$).
* **Remedy:** Reserve $v = (v_1, \dots, v_n)$ exclusively for the speed vector (or $V$ throughout), and define the quotient space using calligraphic or distinct notation: $\mathcal{X}_v := \mathbb{R}^n / \mathbb{R}v$, with quotient torus $\mathbb{T}_v^{n-1} := \mathcal{X}_v / \pi(\mathbb{Z}^n)$, and norm $\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{X}_v}$.

### 2. Missing $\|\sigma\|$ Term in Branch-and-Bound Upper Bound $U(C)$
* **Location:** Page 13, §5.3, displayed equation for $U(C)$.
* **The Gap:** The paper defines $U(C)$ on a dyadic $c$-box $C$ as:
  $$U(C) = \min_{\sigma \in [0, 1)} \max_j \sup_{c \in C} \|c_j - v_j \sigma\|$$
  Notice that **$\|\sigma\|$ is missing from the outer maximum**, unlike Eq. (1) on Page 5:
  $$D(c) = \min_{\sigma \in [0, 1)} \max\left(\|\sigma\|, \max_{j \ge 2} \|c_j - v_j \sigma\|\right)$$
  If $j$ only runs over $2 \le j \le n$, $U(C)$ drops the coordinate of the first runner ($v_1 = 1, c_1 = 0$), making $U(C)$ strictly smaller than $D(c)$ whenever $\|\sigma\|$ dominates, which violates the minimax bound $\max_C D \le U(C)$.
* **Textual Evidence of Inconsistency:** Two sentences later, the text states:
  $$\text{“The }\sigma\text{-search is restricted to }[0, r] \cup [1-r, 1)\text{, which is lossless because any certifying }\sigma\text{ has }\|\sigma\| \le r\dots\text{”}$$
  This proves the algorithm internally enforces $\|\sigma\| \le r$, but the displayed equation forgot to write $\|\sigma\|$.
* **Remedy:** Either explicitly write $\max\left(\|\sigma\|, \max_{j=2}^n \sup_{c \in C} \|c_j - v_j \sigma\|\right)$ or specify that $j$ ranges over $\{1, \dots, n\}$ with $c_1 \equiv 0$ and $v_1 = 1$.

### 3. Variable Collision in Pair-Sum Bad Sets ($w$ as Pair Member vs. Index)
* **Location:** Page 7 (§3.2, line 4) and Page 11 (§4.2, line 2).
* **The Gap:** The text writes:
  $$\text{“For each unordered pair } \{u, w\}\dots \text{some pair's bad sets } B_w = \{k \in \mathbb{Z}_N : (n+1)\|wk\|_N < N\} \text{ fail to cover } \mathbb{Z}_N\text{”}$$
  Here, $w$ is defined as one of the two runners forming the pair $\{u, w\}$ (where $N = v_u + v_w$). But $B_w$ indexes the bad set by runner $w$. The bad sets on $\mathbb{Z}_N$ are generated by the *other* runners $p \in [n] \setminus \{u, w\}$.
* **Remedy:** Change the index to $p$ or $j$:
  $$B_p = \{k \in \mathbb{Z}_N : (n+1)\|v_p k\|_N < N\}, \quad p \in [n] \setminus \{u, w\}.$$

### 4. Under-specified $(n-1)$-Runner Instance $\tau(u, w)$ in Theorem 4.1
* **Location:** Page 10, §4.1.
* **The Gap:** Theorem 4.1 defines $\tau(u, w)$ as "the optimal loneliness of the $(n-1)$-runner instance obtained by restricting to the pair-sum lattice of denominator $N = v_u + v_w$."
* **Impact:** For readers outside the program's companion monograph, this definition is not self-contained. The exact discrete optimization problem needs to be explicitly written out:
  $$\tau(u, w) := \max_{k \in \mathbb{Z}_N} \min_{p \in [n] \setminus \{w\}} \left\| \frac{v_p k}{v_u + v_w} \right\|.$$
  The text should explicitly state that because $v_u + v_w = N$, $\|v_u k / N\| = \|-v_w k / N\| = \|v_w k / N\|$, meaning runners $u$ and $w$ achieve identical distances at all lattice points $t = k/N$, collapsing the $n$ distances to $n-1$.

### 1. Exact Verification of Headline Witness $c = (1/32, 15/32, 3/32, 7/8)$ for $v = (1, 2, 3, 4, 5)$
* In $c$-coordinates: $c_2 = 1/32, c_3 = 15/32, c_4 = 3/32, c_5 = 7/8$.
* Convert to common denominator $288$:
  $$c_2 = \frac{9}{288}, \quad c_3 = \frac{135}{288}, \quad c_4 = \frac{27}{288}, \quad c_5 = \frac{252}{288}.$$
* The claim asserts the minimum in Eq. (1) is attained at $\sigma = 31/288$:
  * $\|\sigma\| = \frac{31}{288}$.
  * $\|c_2 - 2\sigma\| = \left|\frac{9 - 62}{288}\right| = \frac{53}{288} \le \frac{97}{288}$.
  * $\|c_3 - 3\sigma\| = \left|\frac{135 - 93}{288}\right| = \frac{42}{288} \le \frac{97}{288}$.
  * $\|c_4 - 4\sigma\| = \left|\frac{27 - 124}{288}\right| = \left|-\frac{97}{288}\right| = \mathbf{\frac{97}{288}}$.
  * $\|c_5 - 5\sigma\| = \left|\frac{252 - 155}{288}\right| = \frac{97}{288} = \mathbf{\frac{97}{288}}$.
* At $\sigma = 31/288$, runners 4 and 5 achieve opposing slopes ($+4$ and $-5$). Any local shift in $\sigma$ strictly increases $\max(\|c_4 - 4\sigma\|, \|c_5 - 5\sigma\|)$.
* **Result:** $D(c) = 97/288 > 96/288 = 1/3$. **The witness is mathematically airtight.**

### 2. Exact Verification of Ehrhart Polynomial and $h^*$-Vector for $n = 3$

```latex
  $\delta(V) = \operatorname{dist}_\infty\left(\mathbb{R}V, \mathbb{Z}^n + \frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1}\right)$.
  The elementary identity $\delta = \frac{1}{2} - \lambda$ follows from 
  $\max_j \|\frac{1}{2} - v_j t\| = \frac{1}{2} - \min_j \|v_j t\|$.
  \item \textbf{Quotient-torus form.} Let $\mathcal{X}_V = \mathbb{R}^n / \mathbb{R}V$ be the quotient vector space, 
  $\pi : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathcal{X}_V$ the projection, $\Lambda = \pi(\mathbb{Z}^n) \cong \mathbb{Z}^{n-1}$ the projected lattice, 
  and $\|c\|_{\mathcal{X}_V} = \inf_{x \in \pi^{-1}(c)} \|x\|_\infty$ the quotient norm. 
  Then $\delta(V) = \operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{X}_V}(c^*, \Lambda)$, where $c^* = \pi(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{1})$ is the center class 
  (with coordinates $1/2$ on even speeds and $0$ on odd speeds in standard $c$-coordinates with $v_1 = 1$). 
  The $\delta$-form asserts: $\|c^* - \Lambda\|_{\mathcal{X}_V} \le \mathrm{thr}_n$ for every $V$.
  \item \textbf{Covering-radius form ($\rho$-form).} Let $\rho(V) = \max_{c \in \mathcal{X}_V} \operatorname{dist}_{\mathcal{X}_V}(c, \Lambda)$ 
  be the covering radius of $\Lambda$ in the quotient norm. The $\rho$-form asserts $\rho(V) \le \mathrm{thr}_n$ for all $V$.
\end{enumerate}

Forms (1)--(3) are equivalent to $\mathrm{LRC}_n$. Form (4) is strictly stronger: since $\delta(V) \le \rho(V)$ always, 
$\rho(V) \le \mathrm{thr}_n \implies \delta(V) \le \mathrm{thr}_n$.
```

### 3. Formalizing Theorem 4.1 (Sum-Only Equality)

```latex
\begin{theorem}[Sum-only equality; Theorem 1.1 of~\cite{companion}]
Let $V = (v_1, \dots, v_n)$ have distinct positive integer entries with $\gcd(V) = 1$ and $0 < M(V) < \frac{1}{2}$. 
For each unordered pair of indices $\{u, w\} \subset [n]$, let $N_{u,w} = v_u + v_w$ and define the pair-sum lattice loneliness:
\begin{equation}
\tau(u, w) := \max_{k \in \mathbb{Z}_{N_{u,w}}} \min_{p \in [n] \setminus \{w\}} \left\| \frac{v_p k}{v_u + v_w} \right\|.
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
M(V) = \max_{\{u, w\} \subset [n]} \tau(u, w).
\end{equation}
Consequently, $\mathrm{LRC}_n$ holds for all $V$ if and only if for every $V$, there exists a pair $\{u, w\}$ 
such that $\tau(u, w) \ge 1/(n + 1)$.
\end{theorem}
```
