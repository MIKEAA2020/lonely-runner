1. THE C-HALF MADE ARITHMETIC (the advisor's three steps)
----------------------------------------------------------
Setup.  S a core, |S| = k, gap(S) >= 1/(k+1); x killed-but-not-clean (a
dropper); V = S u {x}; t* = a/b (lowest terms) a crossing argmax of V with
binders u != w, p = q = g_new.  At a crossing x itself binds (p = g_new),
so the binder pair is (x, v) for some v in S.

STEP 1 (THEOREM, residue law; proved in the drop audit).  b | (x + v) or
b | (x - v).  Measured on 24,216 crossing binder pairs: sum-law (x + v = 0
mod b) 22,318 = 92.2%, difference-law 1,898 = 7.8%, no exceptions.  The
quotient j = (x +- v)/b satisfies j = 1 in 64.9% of pairs (b = x + v
exactly), j <= 2 in 80.1%.  j is the "overshoot index" of the filler.

STEP 2 (THEOREM, the pole law; NEW proof below).  Write m := C* * b, an
integer (C* = ||v a/b|| = dist_b(va)/b).  Then the residues
    R(t*) := { (u a) mod b : u in V }
satisfy  min R = m  and  max R = b - m  EXACTLY: some binder sits at m
(rising through t*) and some binder at b - m (falling).  Equivalently, the
circular gap sequence of the point set {0} u R starts and ends with gap
exactly m.  Verified 20,804/20,804 and on every patch crossing.

    Proof.  Binders have residue in {m, b-m}.  Suppose all binders have
    residue b - m (falling side: for t < t*, ||v t|| = g_new + v(t*-t) >
    g_new).  Every non-binder has ||u t*|| > g_new, hence by continuity
    ||u t|| > g_new on a neighborhood of t*.  So q_V(t*-eps) > g_new for
    small eps > 0, contradicting global maximality of t*.  The all-rising
    case is symmetric (look at t* + eps).  So the slopes are mixed: a
    binder at m and a binder at b - m.  Since every u in V has
    ||u t*|| >= g_new, i.e. (u a) mod b in [m, b-m], the two poles are
    attained.  QED.

STEP 3 (the deficit law -- verified, not yet proved).  Define the deficit
    d := m(k+2) - b.
Then the C-half is EXACTLY the statement

    (C-bound)     d >= 0      <=>      b <= m(k+2)      <=>      C* >= 1/(k+2).

VERIFIED: d >= 0 on all 20,804 crossing entries of the drop experiment and
on all crossings of the inter-rung patch scan (Section 5) -- zero
violations.  d = 0 occurs exactly at floor landings (m/b = 1/(k+2)); d = 1
is the second band; the rung family has d = m - 1; the inter-rung
inhabitant 3/23 at n=7 has d = 1 (m = 3, b = 23, k+2 = 8).

Equivalences (elementary).  With R the residues (distinct case; duplicates
merge points and are handled by the multiset version):

    d >= 0  <=>  (b - 2m) <= m k  <=>  average interior residue gap <= m
             <=>  the k+1 residues fit [m, b-m] with mean spacing <= m.

So the C-half says: THE DILATE a*V MOD b IS DENSE-ENOUGH IN Z_b -- the
point set {0} u (aV mod b) has no slack in its average spacing.  This is
the precise form of "the covering at a crossing argmax is efficient"
(Section 3).


2. THE LAW HIERARCHY AT CROSSINGS (what the data says)
------------------------------------------------------
L0  POLE LAW (theorem).  min R = m, max R = b - m.  100% (20,804/20,804).

L1  m-NET LAW.  max circular gap G of {0} u R satisfies G <= m: holds on
    19,640/20,804 = 94.4%.  The 1,164 violations have excess gaps mostly
    +1 (920), decay to +12; the worst all come from one hub core
    ({1,3,4,5,7,12} with large fillers) and all sit far above the floor
    (d >= 19 there).  L1 => the C-bound; L1 fails only where the C-bound
    has slack.

L2  DEFICIT LAW (the C-bound).  d >= 0: 100%.  THE OPEN CORE.

L3  PERFECT-AP STRUCTURE AT d=0.  d = 0 <=> floor landing, and then the
    point set {0} u R is exactly the arithmetic progression m, 2m, ...,
    (k+1)m (with duplicate residues merging adjacent points: the 22/750
    non-uniform rows are duplicate cases).  I.e. at a floor landing the
    dilate a*V mod b IS m * Z_{k+2}.  The tight extensions are the perfect
    nets.

L4  BINDER-COHORT LAW.  The crossing's core binder v is one of the CORE's
    own argmax binders: 22,560/24,216 = 93.2%.  The mixed crossing binds
    the filler against the core's own binding cohort -- the new argmax
    re-uses the core's argmax skeleton.  (The 6.8% exceptions are
    non-binder partners, typically at large j.)

L5  OVERSHOOT LAW (theorem, repackaged).  x is a dropper iff for every
    argmax time a_j/b_j of S:  dist_{b_j}(x a_j) <= gap(S) * b_j - 1.
    For unified-grid cores (86.6% of rows: all argmaxes of S on a single
    denominator b_S with gap(S) = m_S/b_S) this reads: x * J_S inside
    [-(m_S - 1), m_S - 1] (mod b_S), J_S the argmax index set.  Droppers
    are EXPLICIT RESIDUE CLASSES mod b_S: consecutive cores (J = units):
    x = 0 mod (k+1); {1,4} (J = {2,3}, m_S = 2): x = +-2 mod 5; the
    tight 6-core {1,2,3,4,5,7}: x = 0 mod 6.  The signed residue eps of x
    mod b_S satisfies |eps| <= m_S - 1 ALONG J_S (not along 1).

L6  GRID LAW.  All argmaxes of the core share one reduced denominator
    b_S: 454/454 cores of size >= 2 in the drop universe were unified
    modulo 20 primitive exceptions of the 524 (3.8%) -- and at census
    scale ([1..18], all sizes): ~94.8% of primitive sets.  The exceptions
    are multi-binder argmaxes at grid coincidences (e.g. {1,4,8}: argmaxes
    1/3 = 3/9 = 4/12 sit on the binder-sum grids 9 = 1+8 and 12 = 4+8
    simultaneously).  The universal statement is the raw candidate
    theorem: every argmax lies on the sum/difference grid of a binding
    pair.  GRID is a zoo-core property, not a universal law.


3. WHY COUNTING CANNOT PROVE IT (the factor-2 wall, made precise)
------------------------------------------------------------------
COVERING LEMMA (theorem).  At a crossing argmax t* = a/b with value m/b,
every grid point i in Z_b has some u in V with dist_b(i u) <= m (since
q_V(i/b) <= m/b).  The speed u covers #{i : dist_b(iu) <= m} =
gcd(u,b) * (2*floor(m/gcd(u,b)) + 1) <= 2m + 1 indices.  Hence

    b <= (k+1)(2m + 1)        (the counting bound).

Proof: sum the covered counts over the k+1 speeds.  QED.

The C-bound asks for b <= m(k+2) ~ m(k+1) -- exactly HALF the counting
bound.  The C-half is the assertion that the covering forced by the argmax
is factor-2 EFFICIENT (near-no-overlap in the index direction), which no
counting/measure argument can deliver: the measure version gives
gap >= 1/(2n) vs the conjectured 1/(n+1) -- the same factor 2, i.e. the
C-half restricted to drops IS the factor-2 problem of LRC in disguise.
What can still work is ALGEBRA: zoo cores are sum-rich near-APs, their
dilates a*V mod b stay near-APs (L3: perfect APs exactly at the floor),
and the deficit measures the deviation from AP.  The proof target is:

    (NET FORM)  at a crossing argmax, the point set {0} u (aV mod b)
    has average interior gap <= m, equivalently d >= 0.

The empirical support: L1 (94.4% max-gap <= m), L3 (perfect APs at d = 0),
and the (m,d) structure: d = 0 exactly at the floor, d = 1 in the second
band (m = 1: value 1/(k+1), 688 entries; m = 2: rung-2, 184; m = 3: the
inter-rung 3/23, exactly 2 entries at aug = 7 -- the committed census
vector), rung family d = m - 1 (1,668 of 7,528 rung-form entries; the rest
are descaled rung values with larger d).


4. EXHAUSTIVENESS (the second directive: the verdict)
-----------------------------------------------------
Universe: EVERY subset of [1..18], sizes 2..7 (63,003 sets + 18 singleton
cores), exact gaps; productivity of every (N-1)-subset via the General
Filler Theorem (X(S, 1/(k+2)) != {}, unbounded filler) for P1 [tight],
plus an x <= 36 landing-on-ladder scan for P2 [tight or rung at size k+1].
"V is an extension" := some (N-1)-subset of V is productive.  "Seed" :=
no productive subset.  "Strict seed" := additionally every removal strictly
raises the gap (V is a drop from every core -- the reduction-relevant
case).  "Band" := gap(V) < 1/N (below the previous floor: the range where
decomposition is needed).

FINDING E-1 (full exhaustiveness is FALSE).  P2-seed rates: N=4: 1,123/
3,060 = 37%; N=5: 7,464/8,568 = 87%; N=6: 17,245/18,564 = 93%; N=7:
31,064/31,824 = 97%.  Most sets are NOT extensions of productive cores.
The program does NOT decompose the whole universe -- and it does not need
to (E-2, E-3).

FINDING E-2 (BAND EXHAUSTIVENESS -- the version the reduction needs).
Every set with gap < 1/N has a productive core:

    N=2: 106/106   N=3: 64/64   N=4: 50/50   N=5: 22/22
    N=6: 20/21     N=7: 14/14

with the SINGLE exception at N = 6:

    V = {1, 5, 6, 11, 16, 17},  gap = 5/33,

a strict seed sitting in the inter-rung window (1/7, 2/13).  Its core
{1,5,6,11,16} (gap 4/17, a descaled rung-4) is zoo-productive ONLY via the
inter-rung extension x = 17 (5/33): under tight/rung productivity it is
unproductive, under FULL-ZOO productivity (tight + rung + inter-rung,
recursive) it is productive and the exception disappears.

FINDING E-3 (THE SEED-BAND LAW).  gap(seed) >= 1/N -- seeds never sink
below the previous floor: verified at N = 2..5 and N = 7 (min seed gap =
1/N exactly, attained by descaled-tight plateaus); at N = 6 the unique
violator is the inter-rung inhabitant (5/33 < 1/6), i.e. exactly E-2's
exception.  Seed gaps concentrate on the (N-1)-ladder values (N=5: 2/9 x73,
3/13 x92, 4/17 x106, 1/5 x9; N=6: 1/5 x1032, 2/11 x107, 3/16 x75, 4/21
x65, 1/6 x21, 5/33 x1) -- seeds are touch-plateaus and descaled towers on
the zoo one level down, plus the inter-rung exception.

FINDING E-4 (the corrected reduction).  With the full-zoo productivity
notion:

    LRC(N)  <=  (band-exhaustiveness: every V with gap(V) < 1/N is a
                 full-zoo-productive extension)
              + (C-half for full-zoo cores)
              + (Q-half).

Sets with gap >= 1/N are automatically safe (>= 1/N > 1/(N+1)).  The n=7
inter-rung inhabitants are covered: {1,2,3,4,5,7,18}'s core {1,2,3,4,5,7}
has the tight extension x=12 (P1-productive); {1,3,4,5,7,13,18}'s core is
P2-productive.  The N=6 inhabitant is the unique set that forces the
inter-rung level into the productive class.

6. WHAT IS PROVED / WHAT IS OPEN (the honest boundary)
------------------------------------------------------
PROVED (this note): the pole law (mixed slopes at any argmax); the covering
lemma and its factor-2 gap (why counting cannot reach the C-bound); the
overshoot law as an iff-package; the equivalence chain C* >= 1/(k+2) <=>
b <= m(k+2) <=> average interior gap <= m; C-bound for consecutive cores
= the rung family theorem (previous note).

VERIFIED, NOT PROVED: the C-bound itself (20,804 + 297 crossings, zero
violations); the m-net law (94.4%); the perfect-AP structure at d = 0; the
binder-cohort law (93.2%); band-exhaustiveness and the seed-band law
(N <= 7, universe [1..18], x <= 36 for the rung part of P2).

The C-half in the drop regime is exactly the factor-2 problem of LRC, now
in three equivalent arithmetic forms (deficit / average gap / net
efficiency).  The evidence says the truth is algebraic (near-AP dilates),
not combinatorial (counts).
