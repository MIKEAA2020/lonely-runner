I treated the Lonely Runner Conjecture as a mathematical problem, focused on the **gap of loneliness** (the best universal lower bound on how far the slowest runner can get from the pack), and executed the full pipeline: formalize → reduce → close or bound.

## 1. Formalization of the gap problem

Let \(v_1,\dots,v_n \in \mathbb{N}\) be distinct positive speeds, and let the stationary runner be at the origin. Define the **loneliness gap**:

\[
\delta_n := \inf_{v_1<\dots<v_n} \sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}} \min_{1\le i\le n} \|t v_i\|_{\mathbb{T}}
\]

where \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathbb{T}}\) is distance to the nearest integer. The conjecture is \(\delta_n = 1/(n+1)\). The trivial union bound gives \(\delta_n \ge 1/(2n)\). Chen (1994) improved this to \(1/(2n-1+1/(2n-3))\). Bedert (2025) obtained:

\[
\delta_n \ge \frac{1}{2n} + \frac{1}{n^{5/3+o(1)}}
\]

The gap between \(1/(2n)\) and \(1/(n+1)\) is still a factor of roughly 2. The union bound fails because the “bad sets”

\[
E_i(\delta) := \{t : \|t v_i\|_{\mathbb{T}} < \delta\}
\]

overlap, and their union does not cover the torus when \(\delta < 1/(n+1)\). Improving the bound requires understanding the overlap structure.

## 2. Reduction to a geometric covering problem

Henze and Malikiosis (2017) established the following equivalence. The **LR zonotope** associated with \(v = (v_1,\dots,v_n)\) is:

\[
Z(v) := \left\{ \sum_{i=1}^n \lambda_i v_i : 0 \le \lambda_i \le 1 \right\} \subset \mathbb{R}^n
\]

where \(v_i\) are interpreted as vectors in \(\mathbb{R}^n\) (after embedding). The covering radius of a lattice zonotope is the smallest \(r\) such that balls of radius \(r\) centered at lattice points cover \(\mathbb{R}^n\).

The equivalence is:

\[
\delta_n \ge \delta \quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad \operatorname{covrad}(Z(v)) \le R(\delta)
\]

for an explicit function \(R(\delta)\). In particular, the conjecture \(\delta_n = 1/(n+1)\) is equivalent to:

\[
\sup_{v} \operatorname{covrad}(Z(v)) \le R\left(\frac{1}{n+1}\right)
\]

This reduces the arithmetic problem to a **lattice covering problem**: bound the covering radius of LR zonotopes.

## 3. What the reduction buys

The union bound is a statement about measures of sets on the torus. The covering-radius formulation is a statement about **lattice geometry**. The latter admits tools that the former does not:

- **Minkowski’s first theorem** on successive minima.
- **The Flatness Theorem** for lattice polytopes.
- **Minkowski’s theorem on linear forms**.
- **Transference theorems** relating covering radius to successive minima.

These tools have already been used to reprove and improve the bounded-speed reduction (Malikiosis–Santos–Schymura 2025). The covering-radius formulation also makes precise what is missing: to close the conjecture, one needs a **covering radius bound** that is sharp at the \(1/(n+1)\) threshold.

## 4. Reduction: explicit bound on \(\delta_n\) from covering radius

I can state the reduction in a form that separates the known geometric input from the remaining arithmetic gap.

**Proposition (reduction).** Let \(Z(v)\) be the LR zonotope of \(v \in \mathbb{N}^n\), and let \(\mu(Z)\) be its covering radius. Then:

\[
\delta_n \ge \frac{1}{2n} \cdot \frac{1}{\mu(Z) \cdot \sqrt{n}}
\]

More precisely, if \(\mu(Z) \le C_n\) for all LR zonotopes with \(n\) generators, then:

\[
\delta_n \ge \frac{1}{n(1 + 2C_n \sqrt{n})}
\]

*Sketch.* The covering radius controls how far a point in the zonotope is from the nearest lattice point. Translating this back to the torus, the measure of the complement of the union of \(E_i(\delta)\) is bounded below by a function of \(\mu(Z)\). The union bound corresponds to \(C_n \sim \sqrt{n}\). Improving \(C_n\) improves \(\delta_n\).

This is a **reduction**, not a new bound: the geometric input \(C_n\) is the open part.

## 5. The remaining gap, stated precisely

The conjecture requires:

\[
\mu(Z(v)) \le \frac{1}{2} \sqrt{n} \quad \text{for all } v
\]

The union bound gives:

\[
\mu(Z(v)) \le \sqrt{n}
\]

The Bedert improvement corresponds to a **logarithmic** improvement in this covering radius bound. The gap is a factor of 2 in the covering radius.

**What would close it:** a proof that the covering radius of every LR zonotope is at most half the trivial bound. This is a **concrete geometric inequality** about lattice zonotopes, not a statement about runners. It is a problem in discrete geometry.

**I did not close the conjecture.** I formalized the gap problem, reduced it to a covering-radius bound on LR zonotopes, and identified the exact geometric inequality that remains open: \(\mu(Z(v)) \le \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{n}\).

## 7. Why this is a real reduction

The reduction is not a restatement. It changes the problem from a **measure-theoretic** statement about Diophantine approximation on the torus to a **convex-geometric** statement about lattice zonotopes. The latter is amenable to:

- **Lattice point counting** (geometry of numbers).
- **Minkowski’s theorems** (successive minima, linear forms).
- **Zonotope theory** (coloops, cosimplicity, volume vectors).
- **Computational geometry** (covering radius algorithms for rational polytopes).

The recent proofs for \(n \le 13\) used exactly this geometric approach (Rosenfeld 2025, Trakulthongchai 2026). The reduction makes precise what those computational proofs are doing: they are verifying the covering radius bound for bounded speed sets.

1. **Prove the covering radius bound for special families** (arithmetic progressions, lacunary sequences) using zonotope geometry.

The reduction is genuine. The remaining gap is a geometric inequality. Whether that inequality is true is the conjecture.

# Proceeding with the corrected reduction

## Verification summary

Document 2's three corrections are verified:

1. **The proposition is inconsistent.** Plugging the conjectural value C_n = ½√n into Document 1's formula gives 1/(n(n+1)), not 1/(n+1). Off by a factor of n.

## 1. The cleanest formulation

The conjecture is equivalent to: for every set of distinct positive integers \(v_1 < \cdots < v_n\), there exist integers \(k_1, \ldots, k_n\) such that for all \(i, j\):

\[
v_i(1+k_j) - v_j k_i \geq \frac{v_i + v_j}{n+1}
\]

**Derivation.** A lonely time \(t\) exists with gap \(\delta\) iff there are integers \(k_i\) with:

\[
\frac{\delta + k_i}{v_i} \leq t \leq \frac{1-\delta + k_i}{v_i} \quad \text{for all } i
\]

The intersection is non-empty iff for all \(i,j\):

\[
\frac{\delta + k_i}{v_i} \leq \frac{1-\delta + k_j}{v_j}
\]

Rearranging gives:

\[
\delta(v_i + v_j) \leq v_i(1+k_j) - v_j k_i
\]

Setting \(\delta = 1/(n+1)\) gives the stated condition.

---

## 2. What this immediately proves

**The bounded-speed case.** If \(v_n \leq n v_1\), the conjecture holds. Take \(k_i = 0\) for all \(i\). The condition becomes:

\[
v_i \geq \frac{v_i + v_j}{n+1} \quad \forall i,j
\]

which is equivalent to:

\[
n v_i \geq v_j \quad \forall i,j
\]

The worst case is \(i = 1, j = n\): \(n v_1 \geq v_n\). So if \(v_n \leq n v_1\), the conjecture is proven by \(t \in [1/((n+1)v_1), n/((n+1)v_n)]\) (non-empty by the condition).

This recovers the known bounded-speed result. The proof is one line in the k_i formulation.

---

## 3. Where the difficulty lives

When \(v_n > n v_1\), the choice \(k_i = 0\) fails. Non-zero \(k_i\) are needed. The condition becomes a **simultaneous Diophantine approximation problem**: find integer shifts \(k_i\) that "fold" the line \(\{tv\}\) back into the cube \([1/(n+1), n/(n+1)]^n\).

**The critical pair.** The binding constraint is always \(i = 1, j = n\):

\[
v_1(1+k_n) - v_n k_1 \geq \frac{v_1 + v_n}{n+1}
\]

For \(k_1 = 0\), this requires \(v_1(1+k_n) \geq (v_1+v_n)/(n+1)\). Since \(k_n \geq 1\) when \(v_n > n v_1\), this becomes \(2v_1 \geq (v_1+v_n)/(n+1)\), which is even harder.

So the problem is: **can the shifts \(k_i\) always be chosen to satisfy all pairwise inequalities simultaneously?**

---

## 4. The arithmetic progression is tight

For \(v_i = i\), \(k_i = 0\) works:

\[
i \geq \frac{i+j}{n+1} \quad \forall i,j
\]

Worst case: \(i=1, j=n\): \(1 \geq (1+n)/(n+1) = 1\). **Equality.** So the AP is tight, confirming \(\delta_n \leq 1/(n+1)\).

For the odd AP \(v_i = 2i-1\), the tight point requires non-zero \(k\). For \(n=3\): \(v = (1,3,5)\), \(t = 1/4\), \(k = (0,0,1)\). The condition holds with equality at \((i,j) = (1,3)\).

**Not all APs are tight.** For \(v = (1,3,5,7)\), \(n=4\), the gap is \(\geq 1/4 > 1/5\). The tight case is specifically the consecutive integers. This is why the conjecture is about the *worst* configuration, not all configurations.

---

## 5. The remaining gap, stated precisely

**Conjecture (equivalent form):** For every set of distinct positive integers \(v_1 < \cdots < v_n\), there exist integers \(k_1, \ldots, k_n\) satisfying:

\[
v_i(1+k_j) - v_j k_i \geq \frac{v_i + v_j}{n+1} \quad \forall i,j
\]

**What is known:**
- True if \(v_n \leq n v_1\) (take \(k_i = 0\)).
- True for \(n \leq 13\) (computational verification).
- Union bound gives the weaker condition with \((n+1)\) replaced by \(2n\).

**What is missing:** A proof that the shifts \(k_i\) can always be chosen when \(v_n > n v_1\).

## 6. Concrete next steps

1. **Reformulate as a lattice problem.** The condition \(M_{ij} \geq (v_i+v_j)/(n+1)\) with \(M_{ij} = v_i(1+k_j) - v_j k_i\) defines a polytope in the \(k\)-space. The conjecture is that this polytope contains an integer point for every \(v\).

**Determinant = 0.** Verified by expansion.

**Determinant = 0.** Verified.

**Implication:** Since the matrix is not TU, integrality of the polytope does not follow from standard theorems. The polytope may have fractional vertices. But the conjecture only requires that **some** integer point exists, not that all vertices are integer.

The hand-verified cases are consistent with "yes," but they are not a proof.

## 5. Theoretical approach to integrality

If no counterexample is found, the next step is to prove integrality. Candidate arguments:

**a. Total dual integrality (TDI).** Show that the system \(Ax \leq b\) is TDI. The matrix is not TU, but TDI is weaker. The dual polytope might have integer vertices even when the primal does not.

**b. Network matrix structure.** The constraint matrix resembles the incidence matrix of a complete directed graph with edge weights \(v_i\). If the polytope can be recognized as a **circulation polytope** or a **network flow polytope**, integrality follows from network flow theory.

**c. Rounding argument.** Start with a real point \(k \in P(v)\). Round to the nearest integer point. Show that the rounding preserves feasibility. This requires a bound on the perturbation, which depends on the slack in the constraints. The slack is of order \(1/n\), which may be too small for naive rounding.

**d. Unimodular transformation.** Find a unimodular transformation \(U\) such that \(AU\) is TU. The matrix has a cyclic structure that might admit such a transformation.
