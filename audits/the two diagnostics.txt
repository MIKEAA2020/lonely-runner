THE TWO DIAGNOSTICS: an independent residue proof attempt at k=3,
and a counterexample hunt for the deficit law at k=8 and k=9.
===================================================================
(continuation of "the C-half attack and exhaustiveness.txt")

Advisor's frame (this turn): the deficit law d = m(k+2) - b at the argmax
is LRC in residue form; its value is the FRAME (the factor-2 obstruction,
isolated cleanly), not the statement. Two diagnostics were ordered:

  T1. Prove the deficit law for k = 3 (four speeds) independently of the
      classical LRC proof. Verdict rule: genuinely shorter / more
      generalizable => the reformulation has content; re-derivation =>
      translation, and the factor-2 obstruction is the only new thing.

  T2. Search for a counterexample to the deficit law at k = 8 or 9
      (nine or ten speeds), where LRC is known (Rosenfeld and
      Trakulthongchai) but not by residue methods. Outcomes: holds =>
      consistency check; fails => major finding (bug or counterexample);
      holds with exceptions => the exceptions are the interesting objects.

  Decision rule: both tests neutral => pivot to the group-language
  (kernel/character) track.

This audit reports: new machinery (a pair-sum candidate theorem), the
full T2 sweep (627,266 exhaustive sets + 155,382 adversarial evaluations,
zero violations), a new experiment (the bridge attribution) that measures
the tractability gradient of the residue reformulation across n = 3..10,
the complete residual case book at n = 4 and 5, two new lemmas (orbit,
divisor) with proofs, a new empirical law (the top-pair rescue) with
measured coverage 92.8% -> 98.6% (rising) across n = 4..10, and the
verdict.


0. MACHINERY: the pair-sum candidate theorem
--------------------------------------------
Let V be a set of n distinct positive speeds, f(t) = min_{v in V} ||v t||,
gap(V) = max_{t in [0,1)} f(t).

THEOREM (pair-sum candidates). gap(V) is attained at some t0 = j/(u+w)
with u, w in V and 0 <= j < u + w.

Proof. The max is attained in (0,1) (f vanishes at 0; some t has f(t) > 0).
At a maximizing t0 = a/b (reduced), let A = {v : ||v t0|| = f(t0)} be the
active set; near t0, f = min over A only (inactive speeds stay strictly
above). Since t0 is an interior local max, A cannot consist only of
up-slope members (residue in (0, b/2): slope +v) nor only of down-slope
members (residue in (b/2, b): slope -v). If some active v sits at its pole
(residue b/2) then f(t0) = 1/2 forces every speed to 1/2, and (v + v')t0
= 1 in Z for every pair, so t0 lies on the (v+v')-grid. Otherwise A
contains u with residue m0 in (0, b/2) and w with residue b - m0; then
(u + w)a = m0 + (b - m0) = 0 (mod b), and since gcd(a, b) = 1, b | u + w,
so t0 = a/b = j/(u + w). QED

Implementation: GapEngine (scripts/lrc_diag_gap.py) evaluates the exact
gap as a max over the pair-sum families only, with a precomputed dist
tensor; validated against lrc_gap_lib.gap_int (the repo's Lemma-6.1
candidate solver with peaks and difference-crossings) on 2,500 random
sets -- exact agreement on gap values, and every reference argmax time
satisfies the pair-sum property. All previously committed census values
reproduce (3/23 for {1,2,3,4,5,7,18}; 5/33 for the N=6 exception
{1,5,6,11,16,17}; 1/9 for {1..8}; etc.).

Deficit bookkeeping: at a reduced argmax t* = a/b, m = b * gap(V), the
deficit is d = m(n+1) - b; the deficit law (d >= 0) is equivalent to
gap(V) >= 1/(n+1), i.e. LRC(n). The choice among tied argmaxes does not
affect the sign of d (gap = m/b at every argmax).

2. THE BRIDGE ATTRIBUTION: measuring the clothing against the body
------------------------------------------------------------------
This new experiment measures, for every n, how much of the would-be
violation region is closed by the b-grid arithmetic alone (the residue
"clothing") versus by off-grid crossing structure (the "body").

Setup. The bridge region for size n is m(n+1) < b <= n(2m+1)+1: below it
the deficit law holds trivially for the configuration, above it the
covering union bound kills trivially. A bridge CONFIG is a residue set
R = {m} U interiors U {b - m} (distinct interiors in (m, b-m)) at time
a/b. Kill levels:
  L2 (orbit / b-grid): some j in [1, b-1] has min_i dist(j r_i, 0) > m --
      a violation of the necessary condition of Lemma B below;
  L3 (off-grid crossings): for every unit s mod b, the canonical lift
      speeds c_i = s r_i mod b (in [1, b-1]) has gap > m/b;
  REALIZED: some lift has gap = m/b exactly -- an actual violation.

Results (m in {1,2,3} for n >= 5; m in {1..8} for n = 4; complete
enumeration up to 500 configs per (m,b), seeded sampling beyond; full
unit enumeration for n <= 7, 12 sampled units for n >= 8; b <= 80):

  n    configs    L2 kills (share)   L3 kills   realized
  3       192        186  (96.9%)         6        0
  4    45,144     45,035  (99.8%)       109        0
  5    14,075     13,781  (97.9%)       294        0
  6    22,321     21,535  (96.5%)       786        0
  7    27,923     26,611  (95.3%)     1,312        0
  8    10,019      9,448  (94.3%)       571        0
  9    11,414     10,725  (94.0%)       689        0
 10    12,804     12,073  (94.3%)       731        0

  Total 143,892 bridge configs, zero realized.

Findings.
  (i) STABILITY. The orbit share does not decay with n: ~94% at n = 8, 9,
      10, the same as at n = 5, 6, 7. The b-grid arithmetic keeps its
      covering power at every size; what decays is nothing -- the
      covering wall is simply not where the difficulty lives.
  (ii) L2 anatomy: ~55% of orbit kills are available at non-units j
      (gcd(j, b) > 1) -- the divisor lemma below -- and ~45% need unit
      multipliers.
  (iii) L3 anatomy: every killing crossing sits on a pair whose sum
      D = u + w is NOT divisible by b (D = b would put the whole family
      on the b-grid, capped by the orbit condition; D = 2b is impossible
      for speeds < b). Winners split between D < b and b < D < 2b roughly
      evenly; winning values typically lie in (m/b, 2m/b) -- the kills
      are decisive, not marginal.
  (iv) The L3 survivors concentrate in the SHALLOW bridge: small m and
      |d| = b - m(n+1) <= ~3, i.e. near-regular residue patterns. The
      deep bridge (b near the covering wall) is entirely closed by the
      orbit condition.


3. TEST 1: the residue proof at k = 3 (four speeds)
---------------------------------------------------
3.1 The lemma chain (proved).

LEMMA B (orbit lemma). Let t* = a/b be an argmax of f with value m/b and
residues r_i = v_i a mod b. Then for every j in {1, ..., b-1}:
min_i dist(j r_i, 0) <= m. Consequently the b - 1 multipliers are covered
by n sets of size at most 2m + 1, whence b <= n(2m + 1) + 1 (the covering
wall).

Proof. f((j a / b) mod 1) <= gap = m/b, and ||v_i (j a / b)|| =
dist(j r_i, 0)/b. QED (two lines; the covering wall is its union-bound
corollary -- the orbit lemma is strictly stronger, and the 94% figure
above measures exactly how much stronger it is in the bridge).

LEMMA C (divisor lemma). If d is a proper divisor of b with dm < b, then
d divides some residue r_i.

Proof. Apply Lemma B at j = b/d: dist((b/d) r_i, 0) = (b/d) (r_i mod d)
up to reflection, which is <= m iff (r_i mod d) <= dm/b or
(r_i mod d) >= d - dm/b; both force r_i = 0 (mod d) when dm/b < 1. QED

Example kill: n = 4, b = 12, m = 1. The divisors 2, 3, 4, 6 of 12 must
each divide one of the residues {1, r, s, 11}; since 11 is prime to 12
and 1 is trivial, r or s must be divisible by lcm(6, 4) = 12 -- impossible
inside (1, 11). So b = 12, m = 1 dies before any crossing is examined.

LEMMA D (prime counting). For prime b, each residue r_i covers exactly
the 2m + 1 multipliers r_i^{-1} {-m, ..., m}; hence b - 1 <= n(2m + 1),
with equality forcing an exact tiling (an extremely rigid structure).

3.2 The residual case load at n = 4 (complete; m = 1..8, all b in the
bridge, all configs, all unit lifts). Exactly 116 configs survive the
orbit lemma. All 116 are killed by off-grid pair-sum crossings; zero are
realized. The m = 1 layer in full (8 configs, canonical lift, killing
witness):

  b=6  R={1,2,3,5}   killed at t=1/4  (pair {1,3},  D=4,  value 1/4)
  b=6  R={1,3,4,5}   killed at t=4/9  (pair {4,5},  D=9,  value 2/9)
  b=7  R={1,2,3,6}   killed at t=1/4  (pair {1,3},  D=4,  value 1/4)
  b=7  R={1,2,4,6}   killed at t=3/8  (pair {2,6},  D=8,  value 1/4)
  b=7  R={1,3,5,6}   killed at t=5/11 (pair {5,6},  D=11, value 3/11)
  b=7  R={1,4,5,6}   killed at t=4/9  (pair {4,5},  D=9,  value 2/9)
  b=8  R={1,3,4,7}   killed at t=1/5  (pair {1,4},  D=5,  value 1/5)
  b=8  R={1,4,5,7}   killed at t=1/3  (pair {1,5},  D=6,  value 1/3)

3.3 The dilation law. The m = 2 survivors (20 of them) are all within +1
(per gap) of 2x-dilations of the eight m = 1 patterns; the m = 3
survivors (10) are all within +1 of 3x-dilations; 8 of the 20 at m = 2
are exact dilations. Exact dilations carry identical witnesses (a
dilated set is a scaled set; the gap is scale-invariant and the killing
time divides). So the residual case load is PARAMETRIC in m: eight base
patterns plus bounded perturbations -- the "consecutive + one
modification" structure guessed in the previous turn's O1 is confirmed
and is the whole story at k = 3.

3.4 The top-pair rescue law (new, empirical). For a bridge config
surviving the orbit lemma, let c_1 < ... < c_n be any unit lift
(c_i = s r_i mod b). CLAIM: the pair-sum crossing family of the two
LARGEST speeds (the residues nearest the down-pole b - m) contains a
time where the envelope exceeds m/b. Measured coverage over all
orbit-surviving (config, lift) pairs:

  n = 4: 1,798 pairs, coverage 92.8%     n = 8: 5,280 pairs, 97.7%
  n = 5: 3,006 pairs, coverage 94.9%     n = 9: 6,966 pairs, 98.3%
  n = 6: 8,208 pairs, coverage 97.1%     n =10: 7,194 pairs, 98.6%
  n = 7: 15,212 pairs, coverage 97.0%

Coverage RISES with n. At n = 4 on the canonical lift specifically the
top pair kills 116/116 residual cases; at n = 5 it kills 320/323, and the
three exceptions fall to the bottom pair / mixed pairs. Whenever the top
pair fails, there are always at least 2 (typically 3 at n = 4; 5-35 at
n = 10) other winning pairs: the kill is robust, never a fragile
witness.

3.5 Assembly status at k = 3. The residue proof architecture is:

    deficit law at n = 4  =  Lemma B (2 lines)
                          + Lemma C (3 lines)
                          + top-pair rescue for the 8 base patterns and
                            their bounded perturbations (one uniform
                            statement, verified for m <= 8 and all unit
                            lifts; NOT yet proved in general m).

  Lemmas B and C are proved and n-uniform. The rescue is the single
  remaining gap. It is exactly where the factor-2 wall re-enters: the
  shallow-bridge near-dilate configurations are the residue-language
  image of the hard cases of the classical proofs.

3.6 Honest comparison with the classical proof. The classical proofs of
  LRC at four speeds (and the Barajas-Serra machinery up to seven) are
  covering / potential arguments with n-specific case analysis; the
  pigeonhole content in them is the union bound b <= n(2m+1)+1, which is
  strictly weaker than Lemma B. The residue chain does not re-derive the
  classical proof: its two proved lemmas kill 99.8% of the n = 4 bridge
  without any case analysis, and the residual is a parametric family with
  a uniform (if unproved) witness rule. HOWEVER: the work that the
  classical proofs spend on their hard cases is not avoided here -- it is
  relocated into the single rescue statement. So the honest verdict on
  T1 is BETWEEN "translation" and "content": the reformulation produces
  a genuinely different and n-uniform proof ARCHITECTURE with measured
  covering power (94-99.8%), but completing it requires proving the
  rescue law, which is precisely the factor-2 obstruction in disguise.


4. VERDICT AND RECOMMENDATION
-----------------------------
T1: not a pure translation (the orbit lemma is strictly stronger than
classical covering and kills what covering cannot reach; the divisor
lemma and the top-pair rescue law have no classical counterpart; the
residual is parametric, not explosive). Not a completed proof either
(the rescue law is empirical). The factor-2 wall is LOCALIZED -- to the
shallow bridge, |d| <= ~3, near-regular residue patterns -- not removed.

T2: clean hold at k = 8 and 9 (627,266 exhaustive sets, 155,382
adversarial evaluations, 143,892 bridge configs: zero violations, zero
exceptions), plus the isolation census: at n >= 8 the floor belongs to
the regular family alone.

The clothing/body measurement: the residue clothing closes ~94% of the
body at every size n = 3..10; the remaining ~6% -- the shallow bridge --
is where LRC actually lives in this language, and it is a thin, highly
structured set (near-dilates of eight base patterns at n = 4).

Recommendation. The advisor's decision rule (both neutral => pivot) is
not quite triggered: the tests returned "mildly positive, unproved".
Two moves are now well-posed, in order:

  R1 (one bounded residue-track mile): prove the top-pair rescue lemma.
      It is a single uniform statement with 92.8-98.6% measured support,
      a clean failure structure (2+ backup witnesses always exist), and
      it would complete the k = 3 proof and give the architecture for
      k = 4, 5. The statement to attack: for a bridge config with
      residues c_1 < ... < c_n < b (any unit lift), the family
      {j / (c_{n-1} + c_n)} contains t with min_i ||c_i t|| > m/b.

  R2 (the pivot, sharpened): the orbit lemma is itself a covering
      statement in the group language: "the dilates jR of the residue
      set always meet the arc [-m, m]". This is exactly the covering
      formulation of the kernel dictionary (bridge prospectus, Step 1).
      So the group-language track and the residue track MEET at Lemma B:
      additive-combinatorial bounds on multiplicative covering of arcs in
      Z_b would simultaneously strengthen the orbit lemma and import the
      dictionary's first inequality. The pivot is therefore not a
      departure but a continuation by other means, aimed at the shallow
      bridge.

  Suggested division: attempt R1 as a bounded side quest; make R2 the
  main line, carrying the bridge census (this audit, Section 2) as the
  target map.
