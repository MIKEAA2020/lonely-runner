Critical mathematical gaps

Theorem 2.1 assumes its conclusion in disguised form. H1g already requires a uniform (k)-growing margin, and H2m supplies a pairwise intersection bound. But the theorem needs a bound on iterated intersections of many differently dilated solution sets. Pairwise sparsity does not control higher-order correlations: every pair can look random while a large common intersection survives. Add an explicit multi-fiber hypothesis, or prove an induction showing why H0 plus H2m prevents correlated survivors.
H0 is not a mathematical hypothesis. “(C_i \approx) …” and “no tangent-drift trajectory stays inside the solution family for many fibers” have no quantifiers, constants, or permitted error. They cannot support a theorem. Replace H0 with an explicit inequality such as
[
|S_{i+1}|\le \Gamma |S_i||\mathrm{Sol}_{i+1}|/p^{m-1}
]
for every admissible history, with fixed (\Gamma), or label the result a heuristic proposition.
The passage from Proposition 9.5 to exhaustion is underproved. The displayed depth estimate controls an independent-extension model, but the actual cascade has dependent size tuples, repeated dilations, resonance fibers, and changing solution sets. The text says these are handled by H0/H2, but never proves that the same recurrence remains valid for the actual history-dependent cascade.
“C(T,k) ≪ |U|” is not enough by itself. A cascade can survive arbitrarily many fibers if the per-step cut is weak or correlated. The paper needs a theorem of the form: every surviving history loses at least a specified fraction before (C(T,k)), not merely an average or pairwise estimate.
The claimed “margin growing linearly in (k)” is not established. The leading depth is essentially (k)-free after cancellation, while (|U|) is linear in (k). That only gives a growing fiber-budget surplus after assuming the depth bound. It does not prove that the actual survivor set decays with the required rate.
The (p=Tk+\epsilon) parametrization is underspecified. (\epsilon) is never given a range or canonical definition, so (k) is nonunique. All statements involving (k), “growth in (k),” the two-value size window, and the asymptotic (ov=(T-6)k+O(1)) need (\epsilon) fixed explicitly, probably (0\le\epsilon<T) or a stated residue class.
H1g is not equivalent to the displayed threshold claim. The bound
[
|\mathrm{Sol}|\le c_1 ov^{m-1},\qquad c_1=O(1)
]
does not imply a useful margin. The proof needs
[
c_1<\left(\frac{T}{T-6}\right)^{m-1}
]
with a quantified margin uniform in (T,k). State that as part of H1g instead of calling it “equivalent.”
H2m’s quantifiers are incomplete. “For every fiber pair” needs to specify whether this includes every size tuple, every anchor solution, every translation (\tau), every difference family, and every prior cascade history. The current wording appears to bound only isolated pair intersections, while the theorem uses conditional intersections after previous filters.
The resonance count is not proved from the definition given. H2r says the number of weak-cut fibers is at most (2\Lambda_0(T-2)/T+1), but (\Lambda_0) is data-chosen and “small-rational (\lambda)” is undefined. Define the resonance set exactly and prove its cardinality, or make H2r an explicit open assumption rather than “derived.”
Lemma 9.3 does not justify the subsequent cut heuristic. The lemma gives a spacing bound through (b_{\min}). The next paragraph jumps from (b_{\min}=1) for small integer (\bar\lambda) to (N\approx(L+1)/\bar\lambda), but (b_{\min}=1) only yields the trivial bound (N\le L+1). A separate arithmetic count is needed for the claimed (1/\bar\lambda) behavior.

**1. Untangle overloaded, multi-clause sentences**
Your arguments are exact, but they are frequently buried in sentences that run to 70 or 80 words. This forces the reader to hold too much context in their working memory.

*   **Improvement:** Break this into declarative steps. 
    * *Proposed:* "The one candidate instrument shaped to produce that missing type was a transfer-matrix or automaton model of the strand census. This was executed as a proof attempt under a pre-committed success criterion, but ultimately closed by theorem (Lemma 9.13). The cascade’s constrained transfer matrices are partial permutations with a spectral radius of exactly 0 or 1 at every scale.
