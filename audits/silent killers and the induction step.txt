SILENT KILLERS AND THE INDUCTION STEP: THE X(S) MACHINERY POINTED AT LRC
=========================================================================

Date: 2026-10-02
Context.  An external assessment of the tight-set decomposition work
("tight-set decomposition via X(S).txt") distinguished progress on the
CLASSIFICATION of tight sets (Problem 1 of the Perarnau-Serra survey)
from progress on the CONJECTURE, and laid out the path between them:

    Step 1  make the X(S) theorem a theorem in general g, not a
            verification on 463 cases;
    Step 2  prove that every set with gap <= 1/(n+1) admits the
            decomposition;
    Step 3  show the decomposition preserves the bound (induction);
    Step 4  universality: all sets decompose.

This audit executes that path as far as it goes, and is explicit about
where it stops.  Summary of outcomes:

    Step 1  DONE.  The filler theorem is general in g and is now stated
            as an exact TRICHOTOMY (gap >, =, < g), proved below and
            machine-checked on 2,865 (S, g, x) triples across regimes
            (lrc_nsk_theory.py NT1).
    Step 2  DONE as a reduction.  Under LRC(m-1): every tight m-set
            decomposes at EVERY removal order (G1), and the sub-threshold
            case reduces to ALL-SILENT sets (Theorem R).  "Every set with
            gap <= 1/(m+1) admits the decomposition" is a theorem whose
            proof needs LRC(m-1); the residue is exactly the conjecture.
    Step 3  The content is the NO-SILENT-KILL property NSC(m): every
            escape-killer touches.  LRC(m) <=> NSC(m) given LRC(m-1)
            (Theorem R).  This audit derives the structural constraints
            on silent killers (L1-L5) and certifies NSC on 1.71 billion
            cores (the NSK census, m = 2..10) -- but the general proof
            of NSC is not here; it IS LRC.  The census pushes the
            certified range to m = 10 speeds (second-largest speed
            <= 40), past the announced nine-speed result.
    Step 4  Subsumed: universality is the induction of Step 3.

Normalization (as in the previous audits): a set of n distinct positive
speeds, gap(V) = sup_t min_{v in V} ||v t||, LRC(n) : gap >= 1/(n+1).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. The General Filler Theorem (trichotomy)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

THEOREM G.  Let S be a finite set of positive integers, g in (0, 1/2)
rational, x an integer with x not in S.  Write g_S(t) = min_{s in S}
||s t||,

    E(S) = { t : g_S(t) > g }   (escape set; finite union of open
                                 intervals (alpha_j, beta_j) when
                                 gap(S) > g, empty otherwise),
    L(S) = { t : g_S(t) = g }   (level set; finite).

Define the two conditions

    (A) [kill]   for every escape interval (alpha_j, beta_j) there is
                 an integer m_j with m_j - g <= x*alpha_j and
                 x*beta_j <= m_j + g  (vacuous when E is empty);
    (B) [touch]  some t in L(S) has ||x t|| >= g.

Then EXACTLY ONE of the following holds, and each is an equivalence:

    gap(S + {x}) > g   <=>   not (A)
    gap(S + {x}) = g   <=>   (A) and (B)
    gap(S + {x}) < g   <=>   (A) and not (B).

An x with (A) and not (B) is a SILENT KILLER of S at level g: it kills
every escape and every level time, and the gap of the extension drops
STRICTLY below the target.  Write D(S, g) = {x : (A)} (the
escape-killers) and X(S, g) = D(S, g) cap {touch} (the fillers).

PROOF.  gap(S + {x}) = sup_t min(g_S(t), ||x t||).

(>)  If (A) fails, some escape interval (alpha, beta) contains a point
     t with ||x t|| > g; there g_S(t) > g as well, so the sup exceeds g.
     If (A) holds, then on E we have ||x t|| <= g (the connected image
     x*[alpha, beta] lies in a single window [m-g, m+g]; windows are
     disjoint since 2g < 1), and off E we have g_S <= g; hence the sup
     is <= g.  This proves the first two lines together: not (A) iff
     gap > g.

(=)  Given (A), the value g is attained iff some t has g_S(t) >= g and
     ||x t|| >= g.  On E, ||x t|| < g STRICTLY in the interior (an
     interior point at window distance exactly g would force an endpoint
     of [x*alpha, x*beta] past a window edge, contradicting (A)), so
     any attaining t has g_S(t) = g exactly: t in L(S).  Conversely a
     level time t with ||x t|| >= g gives min(g, ||x t||) = g there.
     (When E is empty -- gap(S) <= g -- only this clause remains, with
     gap(S + {x}) <= gap(S).)

(<)  Given (A) and not (B): on E the min is < g strictly (interior
     points have ||x t|| < g; endpoints are level times with ||x .|| <
     g by not (B)); on L(S) the min is ||x t|| < g; off E cup L(S) the
     min is g_S < g.  The sup over the compact level-closure is
     attained at a point where the value is < g, hence gap < g.  QED.

The strictness in the third case is what makes the trichotomy exact;
it is also why the touch had to be taken on the FULL level set (the
Task-2 discovery: isolated level times such as t* = 7/15 for H2 carry
the touch).

Consequences (proved in "rung family and the filler theorem.txt", now
in trichotomy form):
  * RADIUS (L1).  (A) forces x*(beta_j - alpha_j) <= 2g for every
    escape, so x <= R(S, g) := floor(2g / L_max).  Escape-killers
    (fillers AND silent killers) live in [1, R(S, g)]; x > R fails (A)
    structurally, so gap(S + {x}) > g for EVERY x > R.  Cores with
    gap(S) <= g have E empty: gap(S) = g gives the infinite family
    {x clean at a level time}; gap(S) < g gives no fillers and every
    extension stays below g.
  * D(S, g) is FINITE and explicitly computable from the escape
    decomposition of S alone -- no gap solver is needed.  This is what
    makes the census below possible.

Machine verification (lrc_nsk_theory.py): NT1 classifies 2,865 random
and fixed (S, g, x) triples (g at LRC levels, rung levels, and off-zoo
values such as 2/5, 3/8; cores above, on, and below the level) with
ZERO mismatches between the trichotomy prediction and gap_int /
lrc_xs_batch.  Silent killers DO exist off the LRC levels -- e.g.
S = {1}, g = 2/5, x = 2 (gap({1,2}) = 1/3 < 2/5) -- which is exactly
the negative control: NSC fails precisely where the level exceeds what
the (m)-sets can support.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. The reduction theorems (the assessment's Step 2)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

COROLLARY G1 (every-order decomposition).  If gap(T) = g and
gap(T \ {x}) > g, then x in X(T \ {x}, g).  In particular, under
LRC(m-1), EVERY tight m-set T decomposes at EVERY removal order: each
element is an X-filler of the remaining core.

Proof: gap(T \ {x}) >= gap(T) = g, and under LRC(m-1) it is >= 1/m >
g, so the trichotomy's middle case applies to the pair (T\{x}, x).  QED.

This upgrades the Task-3 canonical chains (one decomposition per tight
set) to all m decompositions.  Machine check NT2: all 443 (T, x) pairs
of every tight set n = 2..8 (V <= 50) are X-certified with D = X at
every core, zero failures.

THEOREM R (silent-killer reduction of LRC).  Fix m >= 2, g = 1/(m+1),
and let NSC(m) be the statement: for every (m-1)-set S of distinct
positive integers with gap(S) > g, every integer x not in S with (A)
also has (B) (no silent killers; equivalently D = X for every core).
Then:

    (i)  LRC(m)  =>  NSC(m)   (a silent killer exhibits an m-set with
                               gap < 1/(m+1));
    (ii) LRC(m-1) and NSC(m)  =>  LRC(m).

So under LRC(m-1) -- classical for m-1 <= 7 speeds, and now for
m-1 = 8 (Rosenfeld's nine-runner theorem, Jan 2026; independent
Trakulthongchai, announced for nine and ten runners = eight and nine
speeds) -- LRC(m) and NSC(m) are equivalent.  The conjecture IS the
no-silent-kill property, level by level.

Proof.  (i) x silent for S gives gap(S + {x}) < g by Theorem G.
(ii) Let T have gap(T) < g.  If some (m-1)-core S = T \ {x} has
gap(S) <= g, then S violates LRC(m-1) (its bound is 1/m > g), which
is excluded.  So every core has gap(S) > g, and for x = max(T) the
trichotomy puts x in case (A) and not (B): a silent killer, excluded
by NSC(m).  QED.

COROLLARY G2 (counterexample shape).  Let T be a minimal-by-inclusion
m-set with gap(T) < 1/(m+1) (all (m-1)-cores above the level; automatic
under LRC(m-1)).  Then EVERY x in T is a silent killer of T \ {x}.
In particular, by L1:

    every x in T satisfies  x <= R(T \ {x}, 1/(m+1)),

so every (m-1)-subset of a counterexample has all its level-g escapes
of length <= 2g = 2/(m+1) ("near-tight in escape length").  A
counterexample is an ALL-SILENT, mutually near-tight configuration:
each element parks near an integer across every escape of the others
and stays strictly inside g of every integer at every level time of
the others.  The census below enumerates exactly these support cores.

3. Structural constraints on silent killers (the Step 3 attack surface)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

L1 RADIUS.  x silent => x <= R(S, g) = floor(2g / L_max(S, g)).
   Silent killers are SMALL relative to the escape geometry.

L2 PARKING.  For each escape (alpha_j, beta_j) there is an integer
   m_j with x*alpha_j, x*beta_j in [m_j - g, m_j + g]: through every
   escape the filler sits within g of ONE integer.

L3 CONTOUR STRICTNESS.  x silent => ||x t|| < g for EVERY t in L(S, g)
   (strictly), in particular at both endpoints of every escape.

L4 PINNING.  Two escapes parked at integers m_1, m_2 give
   | x*(alpha_2 - alpha_1) - (m_2 - m_1) | <= 2g: x is pinned to the
   Diophantine condition frac(x * Delta) in [0, 2g] union [1-2g, 1]
   for every inter-escape offset Delta.  With several escapes x is
   confined to O(1) values per integer hypothesis -- the mechanism
   behind the tiny X-lists of the zoo.

L5 RESIDUE CONDITION.  At a level time t* = p/d (lowest terms), the
   silent condition ||x t*|| < g restricts x mod d to the residues
   with dist(x*p mod d, 0) < g*d: a modular condition per level time.
   Simultaneous level times with different denominators squeeze x by
   CRT.  (This is Lemma A's mechanism transposed from argmax grids to
   level sets.)

None of these individually eliminates silent killers; together they
make the census cheap and give the eventual proof its shape: an
all-silent set must satisfy L1-L5 at every element simultaneously.

PROPOSITION P (scaling; with an errata).  For all c >= 1:
   c * X(S, g) is a subset of X(c*S, g) (the escape/level structure
   transfers under t -> c*t because ||x*(c t)|| = ||(c x)*t||).
   The inclusion can be STRICT: X({1}, 1/3) = {2} but X({2}, 1/3) =
   {1, 4} -- the extra filler x = 1 is a DESCALING filler ({2} + 1 =
   {1} + 2 = {1, 2}), corresponding to the non-integral rational
   filler x/c = 1/2 of {1}.  ERRATA for the Task-3 atlas: the claim
   "X(c*S) = c*X(S) exactly, 324/324" was measured only on core pairs
   at levels 3..8; at level 2 equality fails as above.  Equality does
   hold on all 324 zoo core pairs at levels 3..8 (re-verified, NT4)
   and on all census productive cores classified below.

4. The no-silent-kill census
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

The census (lrc_nsk_census.c, exact int64 rational arithmetic) walks
every (m-1)-core S in [1..B] at level g = 1/(m+1) and certifies
D(S, g) = X(S, g):

  * probe:  midpoints of the crossing grid of the largest speed
            (crossings of ||s_max t|| = g).  Every escape longer than
            2g contains a probe point (grid gap < 2g), and the exact
            local escape around a probe (nearest crossings of each
            speed, O(m)) recovers that escape.  If no x in
            [1, R_found] \ S kills all FOUND escapes, then D is empty
            (D must kill all escapes and D <= R <= R_found) -- the
            core is SKIPPED with a certificate.
  * full:   near-tight cores (all escapes <= 2g) get the exact event
            scan (events = all level-g crossings of all speeds;
            segment signs at exact midpoints; escapes = maximal
            positive runs; level times = events with g_S = g), then
            D and X for every x in [1, min(R, cap)], cap = 3000.
  * flags:  cores with E empty (gap <= g: would violate LRC(m-1)),
            radii above the cap, and any SILENT killer are flagged.

Validation: 480/480 random cores match the independent Python
reference (V-A); 68 (S, x) trichotomy pairs against gap_int (V-B);
m=4 B=14 census equals the Python enumeration exactly (V-C); the
m=8 B=20 census reproduces the brute-force lrc_xs_batch census of all
203,490 8-sets in [1..21] (tight sets and their cores exactly, zero
sub-threshold sets); after the runs, ALL 2,137 X-fillers of all tiers
were re-verified to give gap exactly 1/(m+1), and 120 sampled
non-killers give gap > g (lrc_nsk_analyze.py).

THE CERTIFIED CLAIMS (Theorem R makes each of these an LRC statement):

  * LRC(m) holds for every m-set T with T \ {max(T)} inside [1..B_m]:
    no such set has gap < 1/(m+1).  The top element needs no bound:
    any counterexample's max is a silent killer, hence <= R <= max_R
    (<= 1000 across all tiers), and every x > R fails (A) structurally.
  * m = 9, B = 50: an independent computational verification of the
    announced nine-speed case (536,878,650 cores; every 9-set with
    second-largest speed <= 50 has gap >= 1/10).
  * m = 10, B = 40: the OPEN frontier.  Every 10-set with
    second-largest speed <= 40 has gap >= 1/11.  (273,438,880 cores;
    no proof exists or is announced at ten speeds.)  The m = 10 tier
    is self-contained: any 9-core at or below 1/11 would have been
    flagged (none was), so the census does not even assume LRC(9).

THE PRODUCTIVE-CORE MAP (near-tight cores with D != empty, i.e. the
supports of tight sets; full lists in out/nsk_*_cores.txt and
nsk_census_report.json):

  * m = 6, 8, 9, 10: the map is EXACTLY the ladder family
    c * ([m] \ {j}) (124, 57, 46, 40 cores).  NO rung-2 seed is
    productive: the n = 6 barrenness of the Task-3 census extends from
    V = 50 to B = 120, the n = 8 barrenness to B = 60, and the
    barrenness now also holds at n = 9 (B = 50) and n = 10 (B = 40).
    Within these universes the tight zoos at n = 6..10 are predicted
    to be the pure scalings c*[n] -- and at n = 9, 10 that is a
    prediction beyond any census taken so far.
  * m = 4, 5, 7: ladder + the known mod-family seeds
    ({1,3,4} -> {2,7}, {3,4,7} -> {1};  {1,3,4,5} -> {2,9},
    {1,4,5,9} -> {3};  {1,2,3,4,5,12} -> {7}, {1,4,5,6,7,11} -> {13})
    + their scalings + the remaining every-order cores of the mod
    families (e.g. {1,3,7} -> {4}, {1,4,7} -> {3} at m = 4;
    {1,2,3,5,7,12} -> {4} at m = 7).  Everything reconciles with the
    Task-3 productivity census; nothing beyond those families appears,
    at B = 200/150/80 respectively (2x-3x beyond the V = 50 horizon).
  * Descaling fillers occur at every level (e.g. {10,15,20,25,30} ->
    X = {5} producing 5*[6]; {6,9,...,27} -> X = {3} at m = 9;
    {6,9,...,30} -> X = {3}, {8,12,...,40} -> X = {4} at m = 10),
    exactly as Proposition P predicts (strictness of c*X(S) in X(cS)).

The census thus re-derives the tight zoos from the core side, extends
the barrenness evidence at four levels, verifies the announced
nine-speed case independently, and certifies the first open-frontier
case (ten speeds) -- all as instances of the single law D = X.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. Honest status
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

What is proved: Theorem G (trichotomy), G1 (every-order decomposition,
given LRC(m-1)), R (the reduction), G2 (counterexample shape), L1-L5,
Proposition P.  What is verified: NSC on 1.71 billion cores across
m = 2..10 with exact arithmetic and triple independent validation.

What is NOT proved: NSC(m) in general for any m >= 2 -- equivalently
(by Theorem R), LRC itself.  The census is finite evidence, and the
structural lemmas constrain but do not eliminate silent killers.  The
path from here to the conjecture remains Step 3: turn L1-L5 into an
impossibility argument for all-silent sets.  The assessment's closing
line stands: the foundation is necessary but not sufficient.

6. Open problems
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

  O1'  Prove NSC(m) for small m directly in the X-language: m = 2
       (LRC(2), classically trivial -- reprove it as "no silent killer
       of a singleton at 1/3"), then m = 3.  A proof of NSC(3) in this
       language would validate the program on the first nontrivial rung.
  O2'  The all-silent rigidity: does G2 + L4 (pinning) + L5 (residues)
       force a contradiction for |T| >= 3?  The census says the
       constraints are mutually unsatisfiable on 1.7e9 cores.
  O3'  Productivity beyond the horizon: continue the m = 9, 10 maps
       (B = 60, 50 at ~2x cost) or attack O1 of the previous audit
       (barrenness as a theorem) with the census's core-side formulation:
       "no core outside the ladder families has D != empty".
  O4'  Inter-rung: the census flags cores by escape length, not by gap
       value; a level-scan variant would probe the inter-rung windows
       at m = 9, 10 (empty at m = 8 through V = 50).
